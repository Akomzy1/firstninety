Day 38 — Week 6 — Mission 2 of 4

Negotiate a scope clarification.

Estimated 25 minutes. Brief / Rehearsal / Reflection.

Why this matters

Scope conversations look easier from the outside than they ever are inside the room. Most juniors avoid them — they read disagreement as conflict and assume someone more senior will eventually intervene. They almost never do. What started as a small ambiguity calcifies into a deliverable nobody actually signed off on, and the person who carries it is you.

Naming a scope question early — when it costs a clarification email — is the difference between a project that ships and one that limps. Do it now and you keep your sponsor's trust. Do it after the deadline slips and you become the person who didn't flag.

What you'll do

First, read the playbook on scope-clarification emails — pay attention to the worked example, where the BA pushes back on a vague brief without sounding combative. Then run the scenario simulator for ten minutes against The Vague Stakeholder; you'll get one round of dialogue and a debrief. Finally, take what you learned into your own situation and draft the one-paragraph clarification email you'll send before end of day.

Playbook Scope Clarification Email Simulator The Vague Stakeholder Coach Talk through your specific situation
Success looks like

You've sent the clarification email and gotten a written response from your stakeholder confirming or refining scope.
