Worked example #2 · regulatory project

BRD — Project Northwind — v1.2

AuthorAdaeze O., BA
SponsorHead of Retail Lending
StatusDraft for review
Last edit14 May 2026
00 On the title block

The codename, the version, and the status are the only three things a reviewer reads before deciding whether to open the document. Naming a project after a colour or a wind isn't whimsy — it protects the real product name from leaking into shared drives during procurement.

01 Executive summary

What we are building, and why now.

Project Northwind delivers a redesigned customer-affordability assessment for unsecured retail lending, replacing a 2019 in-house engine that no longer meets the affordability evidencing requirements set out by the regulator's revised consumer-credit framework. The new assessment is in-app, asynchronous, and producible as a single PDF artefact for audit.

The work is sponsored by the Head of Retail Lending and must be in production for new applications by the regulator's enforcement date. The scope here is the assessment surface and its evidence trail; the underlying credit policy is not in scope and is owned by Risk.

01 On the summary

Three sentences max. If your sponsor reads only this paragraph, they should know what's being built and why now — and crucially, who else owns the bits you're not touching.

Ending the summary by naming what's out of scope stops half the misunderstandings before Section 3 ever has to.

02 Background & business drivers

The Financial Conduct Authority's revised consumer-credit affordability framework (CP24/7, finalised October 2025) requires lenders to evidence the basis on which an affordability decision was made for every credit application, retained for seven years and reproducible on demand. Our existing engine produces a numeric score but no defensible evidence trail; remediation through patching has been costed twice and rejected twice. Enforcement begins on 1 October 2026.

02 On naming the regulator

Name the regulator, the framework, the paper number, and the deadline. Vague regulatory framing — "due to upcoming regulation" — reads as if you don't know what you're talking about, and the compliance reviewer will assume the rest of the document is similarly hand-waved.

03 Scope

In scope.

The customer-facing affordability assessment for new unsecured retail-lending applications across web and the iOS / Android apps. The PDF evidence artefact generated per application, including its retention metadata. Internal review tooling for the Lending Operations team to access an application's evidence on demand.

Out of scope.

Secured lending products. Existing customer in-life reviews — these remain on the legacy engine pending Phase 2. Underlying credit policy and scorecard weights, which are owned by Risk and out of remit. Branch and contact-centre channels, where the assessment is conducted verbally by an agent and follows a separate evidencing process under the same framework.

03 On out-of-scope

List out-of-scope explicitly, in prose. The thing you'll regret most is the requirement someone assumed was included because the BRD didn't say otherwise.

If a competing team owns the adjacent thing, name them — "owned by Risk", "owned by Branch Ops" — so reviewers can route the missing question without coming back to you.

04 Stakeholders

Who decides, who consults, who is informed.

  • SponsorHead of Retail Lending
  • AccountableDirector of Credit Risk Operations
  • Compliance leadSenior Manager, Conduct & Consumer Credit
  • Engineering leadPrincipal Engineer, Lending Platform
  • Design leadStaff Product Designer, Lending
  • Reviewer (consulted)Lending Operations, Internal Audit, Data Protection Office
04 On anonymising names

Use role titles in the document, not real names. Documents travel — to new joiners, to auditors, sometimes to the regulator itself — and named individuals create both a GDPR surface and a stale-document risk the moment somebody moves teams.

Keep the named version in a separate stakeholder log that lives next to the project, not inside the BRD.

05 Requirements

Functional requirements (excerpt).

  • REQ-001The system shall capture income, essential outgoings, and existing credit commitments at the point of application.
  • REQ-002The system shall record the source and timestamp of each affordability data point.
  • REQ-003The system shall generate a per-application PDF evidence artefact within 90 seconds of submission.
  • REQ-004The system shall retain affordability evidence artefacts for a minimum of seven years from decision date.
  • REQ-005Lending Operations users shall be able to retrieve any application's evidence artefact by application reference within two seconds.
  • REQ-006 through REQ-042 — see Appendix A.
05 On the requirement IDs

Notice the IDs. They're not decoration — they're for traceability. Every requirement here should appear in test plans, acceptance criteria, and the traceability matrix that audit will ask for.

The IDs survive the document. Renumber them and you've broken every reference downstream; that's the single most expensive editing mistake on a regulated project.

06 Compliance & audit considerations

Every affordability decision produced under this scope is reportable evidence under CP24/7 §4.2 and must be reproducible on demand for seven years, with no degradation in fidelity. Each PDF evidence artefact is therefore generated from immutable source records and stored under WORM (write-once, read-many) retention; subsequent corrections produce a new artefact with a forward reference, never an overwrite.

Personal data captured in support of an affordability decision is processed under legitimate interest and retained for the seven-year period under regulatory obligation, surviving a customer's right-to-erasure request for that period — a position confirmed in writing by the Data Protection Office on 03 April 2026 and reproduced in Appendix C.

Internal Audit will sample-test fifty applications per quarter for the first four quarters post go-live, then move to an annual sample. The acceptance criteria for the sample test are reproduced in Appendix B and are referenced against requirements REQ-002, REQ-003, REQ-004, and REQ-005.

06 On this section existing at all

This section is the entire reason the regulatory variant exists. Skipping it isn't optional, and condensing it into a paragraph isn't either.

Three things go here, always: how the evidence is produced and stored, how data-protection sits alongside the retention obligation, and who will check the work after go-live. Anything else belongs in an appendix.

If you can't write this section because you don't yet know the answers, that's also useful — it tells you which meetings to book this week.

Common mistakes

Three things new BAs get wrong in regulatory BRDs.

They write Section 2 like a marketing brief. The Background section names the regulator, the paper number, and the deadline — not the customer benefit, not the strategic ambition, not the journey vision. A reviewer reading Section 2 should be able to repeat the regulatory driver back to you in one sentence. If they can't, you've written a different document by accident.

They put compliance considerations in an appendix. Section 6 isn't optional and it isn't supplementary; on a regulatory variant it is the centre of gravity of the document. Burying it at the back signals to the compliance reviewer that you haven't grasped what you're working on, and they will read the rest of the document accordingly.

They renumber requirements between drafts. By v1.2 there are already test plans, acceptance criteria, and a traceability matrix referencing REQ-014. Renumbering it because you've inserted three new requirements above it breaks every downstream artefact silently. Append, never reorder. If a requirement is removed, retire the ID — don't reuse it.

Variant patterns

When the regulatory deadline is hard, the structure tightens.

Move the deadline into the title block. On a hard-dated regulatory project, the enforcement date stops being prose in Section 2 and becomes metadata next to the version number. Every reviewer's first read should surface the date without scrolling.

Collapse scope into a single tighter section. When the timeline is fixed, the conversation about scope is no longer "what could we build" but "what is the smallest thing that meets the obligation." Section 3 becomes shorter and more declarative, and Section 5 carries the weight that scope normally would.

Add a Section 6.1 — what we are deliberately not solving. On a hard-dated project, the temptation to fix adjacent legacy problems is strong, and almost every one of them will pull the deadline. Naming what you are choosing not to fix — and committing to a Phase 2 — is the single most useful sub-section you can add.
