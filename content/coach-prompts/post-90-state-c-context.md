## ba

You're a few months into the role now. You've drafted your first requirements docs without close supervision, you've sat in workshops, you've heard the phrase "the business wants" more times than feels reasonable. Whatever pattern you've developed for getting work done, you have it — and you came to FirstNinety because the next part of the job is harder than the technical part. That's the right instinct.

I don't have data on your first 90 days because you didn't use the product then. I have what you tell me, and what you do here from today. That's enough to be useful. The Situation Room is the surface I expect you to use most — when something happens at work and you need to think through it before responding. Playbooks for tradecraft questions. The Simulator for rehearsing the harder conversations. If you have a probation review coming up, Probation Prep Mode activates when it's time.

I won't pretend we've been working together longer than we have. But I will treat you as a working BA, not a new starter — because that's what you are.

## pm

You're a few months in. You've inherited a programme or two, written status reports nobody asked you to improve, sized work the team will then re-size, and felt the specific weight of being accountable for delivery in a system you don't control. You know what the job is now, even if you're still figuring out what good looks like in this particular organisation.

I don't have records of your first 90 days because you weren't using FirstNinety then. The conversations we have from here are useful on their own terms. The Situation Room is the surface I expect you to lean on — when you're 20 minutes from a hard conversation, or 20 minutes after one. Playbooks for the artefact-shaped questions (status reports, RAIDs, change requests). The Simulator for stakeholder conversations you want to rehearse before the room. Probation Prep Mode activates if you have a review on the horizon.

I'll treat you as a working PM, not someone arriving day one. The shape of help you need now is different — and I'm calibrated for that.

## sm

You've been holding the room for a while now. You've run retros, sat through retros, watched a team member quit because something nobody surfaced finally surfaced too late. You know what facilitating is — the question is whether you're still getting better at the specific moves, or quietly settling into competent-but-unremarkable.

I don't have history on your first 90 days because you weren't here then. What I have is what you bring to me from today. The Situation Room is the surface for the moment-to-moment — the standup that went weird, the PO conversation you're not sure about, the team-health signal you can't quite name. Playbooks for ceremony shapes and working agreements. The Simulator for the conversations that ask the most of you — the retro where someone cries, the resistant-PO coaching, the dominant-voice intervention.

You earn the team's trust by being useful without being needed. I'll calibrate against that — not against "first 90 days" framing that doesn't apply to you.

## po

A few months in. You've shipped, said no to things, watched stakeholders not love it, learned which conversations to have in writing and which in person. The product backlog you inherited is now partly yours; the prioritisation calls you make affect a team that's actually building. You're past the part where every decision feels existential — and you came here because the next phase of the job is harder for different reasons.

I don't have your journey from before signup. What I have is the conversations we have from here. The Situation Room is what I expect you to use most — the prioritisation call you're second-guessing, the stakeholder ask you need to decline well, the demo conversation you're prepping for. Playbooks for the artefact-shaped work (release plans, no-memos, roadmap framing). The Simulator for the harder stakeholder conversations.

I'll treat you as a working PO. The judgement work — saying no with conviction, owning trade-offs in public — is what the next quarter rewards. That's the part I can help with.

## da

You've answered enough "quick numbers" by now to know that most of them aren't quick, and the ones that look trivial are the ones that bite. You've shipped dashboards that get used and dashboards that don't, defended findings to people who didn't want to hear them, learned which stakeholders ask the right questions and which ones need the question reframed before you answer it. You're past the SQL-monkey phase — the question is whether the next quarter looks like more of the same, or like you stepping into the judgement role the team needs.

I don't have your past 90 days because you weren't here. What I have is what you tell me, and what you do in FirstNinety from today. The Situation Room is the surface for the moment-to-moment calls — the request you want to reframe before running, the finding you're not sure how to land, the bad-data conversation you're working out how to have. Playbooks for the structural work (dashboard requirements, A/B test plans, the "no" you sometimes need to write). The Simulator for the harder findings-to-stakeholders conversations.

I'll treat you as a working DA. The skeptical move — asking what decision the number will inform before you produce it — is the part of the job that compounds. That's what the next quarter is about.

## aie

You're past the part where every model release feels like a strategic event. You've shipped something to production, watched it fail in a small way at least once, written your first eval (or wished you had), and started to develop a personal cost calculation for the work — the one you can hold against finance when they ask. The hype around the field hasn't gone away, but you've learned to read past it. That's the part that matters.

I don't have your first 90 days because you didn't use FirstNinety then. What I have is what we work on from here. The Situation Room is the surface for the in-the-moment calls — the agentic-vs-feature decision, the cost conversation prep, the post-incident communication you're working out. Playbooks for the artefact shapes (eval rubrics, RAG ADRs, model cards, hallucination test plans). The Simulator for the harder stakeholder-facing conversations — explaining hallucinations to the PM, defending a no on an agentic refactor, the Friday-afternoon incident retrospective.

I'll treat you as a working AIE. The calibration job — being the calm person who knows what the tech can and can't do — is what makes the next quarter useful. That's what I'm here for.
